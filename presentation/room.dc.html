SCENE 1 · ROUND ACLAIM № 1 · SIGHTED

Everyone flips the same fair coin

Before you flip: what do you expect the pooled line to do?

Tap Flip ten times. Every tap lands on this screen.

The honest statement before flipping is a bet: 50%. The line tells us whether the bet was any good.

SCENE 1 · ROUND B

Everyone flips a mystery coin

Your coin always lands the same way. Which way? Nobody knows — not even you.

Ten flips. Your own line is flat; the room's still says ½.

No luck in this coin at all — only ignorance. Same 50%.

SCENE 1 · ROUND C

Everyone flips their own secret coin

Your phone now holds a coin with a bias you cannot see.

Ten flips again. Same pooled forecast?

Both rounds say 50%. Do you believe the same thing in both?

A coin keeps one kind of secret: which way it leans. Hold that.

SCENE 2 · TWO SOURCES OF UNCERTAINTY✳ AHA MOMENT

Same forecast, different knowledge

One number per participant: the fraction of heads, shadowed on the axis. The room's is their average — ½ in every round.

The second number, ± E(sd), lifts each coin off the axis. Round A rises to the rim. Round B never leaves the axis. Round C: some rise, some stay.

One number cannot tell luck from ignorance — two can.

SCENE 2 · TWO WORDS FOR NOT-KNOWING✳ AHA MOMENT

Knowledge shrinks one kind of bandwidth — not the other

What information removes is systematic (epistemic, ignorance). What survives every fact is statistical (aleatoric, luck).

Dashed: one anonymous bag of flips. Solid: the bookkeeper knows whose flip is whose.

Round A: the paths coincide, luck stays. Round B: the solid path stays on the axis, only a fact not yet learned. Round C: both.

SCENE 3 · ONE NUMBER IS NOT ENOUGH✳ AHA MOMENT

Add one honest number and a shape appears

Odds on the x-axis. Bandwidth — the spread you expect — on the y-axis.

Every fully known coin lands on the same arc.

The mystery coins sit in the corners and pool to the centre. A coin you only know on average lands inside. One number added; a shape appeared.

SCENE 4 · THE BERNOULLI CIRCLECLAIM № 3 · SIGHTED

Known coins live on the rim

Slide p. The dot never leaves the arc.

Every coin you fully know sits on the rim. Every coin you don't sits inside.

Technical aside: the arc length between two beliefs is how well a run of throws can tell them apart.

SCENE 4 · MIXING BELIEFS

Blends fill the half-disk

Believe q₁ with some weight, q₂ with the rest: your belief slides along the chord.

All chords together fill the interior — the half-disk is the space of every honest belief.

Rim: randomness that stays. Depth: ignorance that flips can cure.

SCENE 5 · FROM THE STATE'S OWN POINT OF VIEWCLAIM № 2 · SIGHTED✳ AHA MOMENT

Opposite beliefs become perpendicular beliefs

The pointer: centre to state at angle 2θ.

The needle: the same arrow seen from the state, now at angle θ.

Always-H and always-T become perpendicular.

Add the components, then square. Hold on to that.

ACT II · SCENES 7–13

Light, nature's second example

a lamp · two sheets · a detector · and one photon at a time

the beams already in the room — angles still secret

SCENE 7 · THE LAMP ALONE

One sheet: half, whichever way it turns

A lamp, one striped sheet, a detector.

Turn the sheet anywhere: 50%, 50%, 50%. No angle lets everything through, none stops it.

So a sheet is just a grey filter that halves the lamp? Add a second sheet.

SCENE 7 · TWO PAIRS OF SUNGLASSES✳ AHA MOMENT

Two sheets, one strange result

Sheet 1 halves the lamp, as before. Now a second sheet behind it.

With the same stripes it costs nothing more. Turn it crosswise and the detector goes dark. A grey filter would not care.

So light has an H and a T too: behind a horizontal sheet it says yes to “horizontal?” every time — always-H; behind a vertical one, always-T. A known coin, made of light. One more sheet first.

SCENE 7 · A THIRD SHEETCLAIM № 2 · CAUGHT · № 4 · SIGHTED

Light back through an obstacle

Sheet 1 horizontal, sheet 2 vertical: crossed, 0%.

Slide a third striped sheet between them, diagonal: 25%. An extra obstacle, and light comes back.

A grey filter could never do this. Something in the light has a direction, and the middle sheet turned it. Why — the next two slides.

The middle sheet turned the direction — by throwing three quarters of the light away.

SCENE 7 · WHAT IS POLARIZATION?

Polarization is a sideways wiggle — two sizes, one lock

Light is a wave that wiggles sideways as it flies: an H wiggle of size a and a V wiggle of size b, both at once, locked by a² + b² = 1.

That pair (a, b) is all polarization means. A sheet keeps only the wiggle along its slots: fraction a² of the brightness passes.

Insert a sheet half-way: it keeps only the wiggle along its slots — cos of the angle as long, cos² as bright.

SCENE 7 · THE CURVECLAIMS № 1 · № 2 · CAUGHT

Brightness against the angle between them

Only the angle between the wave and the slots matters: φ − β.

The sheet keeps cos(φ − β) of the wiggle; the detector, which reads squares, sees cos²(φ − β).

Aligned: everything. Crossed: nothing. 45° apart: exactly half — the coin's ½, back in glass.

SCENE 8 · ROUND A, IN LIGHT

The fair beam, one photon at a time

Everyone holds the same beam, tilted 45°: as much H as V. Each photon meets the sheet — a click or no click, 1 or 0.

Click by click, every beam draws its path — and the room climbs the rim to (½, ½), as it did with the coin.

Same beam, same sheet, for everyone — the fair beam.

SCENE 8 · ROUND B, IN LIGHT

The mystery beam, one photon at a time

Every seat holds a beam that is either H or V — fixed, and secret. Same horizontal sheet.

An H beam clicks every time; a V beam never does. Each path shoots to a corner — and the room pools to the floor at ½.

No luck anywhere, only a fact nobody knows — the mystery beam.

SCENE 8 · ROUND C, IN LIGHT

The secret beam, one photon at a time

Every seat holds a beam with its own secret tilt — nobody has the same one. Same horizontal sheet.

Each beam clicks its own fraction of the time: every path climbs to its own point on the rim. The room pools inside.

One sheet, three rooms: rim, floor, inside — the secret beam.

SCENE 8 · RECAP ✳ AHACLAIM № 3 · CAUGHT

Turn the sheet 45° — the disk turns 90°

Solid: the room through the 0° sheet. Dashed: through the 45° sheet.

Turn the sheet 45°: the pointer sweeps 90° and lands on the dashed one.

The half-disk was folded. Bandwidth has a sign; the 45° sheet reads it.

A coin took one question; light takes a whole dial — more room for a secret.

SCENE 11 · ASK A DIFFERENT QUESTION✳ AHA MOMENT

Our second number — with a sign

Through the 0° sheet the room reads (p, E(sd)). Turn the sheet 45° and the disk turns 90°.

So the 45° sheet reads (E(sd) + ½, ½ − p): its probability is our bandwidth, plus a half.

Subtract ½ from the 45° answer and you hold E(sd) — sign included. Nature keeps our books, and signs them.

SCENE 12 · THE MIRROR TWINS✳ AHA MOMENT

Half of what? The other half was the sign

Every beam has a triangle over the T–H diameter: legs √p and √(1−p), the numbers the 0° sheet reads. Its twin below has the same legs — and the opposite turning.

The turning is the sign of E(sd). The 0° sheet cannot see it; the 45° sheet reads it (scene 11). Dropping it folded the circle into our half-disk.

The fair beam at 45° sits on top; the 135° beam is its twin — same ½ through the 0° sheet, 100% against 0% through the 45°. Doubt (ii): half of the Bernoulli circle.

SCENE 13 · 1 OF 2 · MEASURING IS ASKING

Every sheet is a diameter; every photon is asked ‘which end?’

A sheet at angle φ is a diameter at 2φ. Its two ends: the beam that always passes, the beam that never does. A photon is never asked where are you? — only which end?

The odds are drawn: the squared chord to the opposite end. That is Malus’s cos²(φ−β) from scene 7, as triangle geometry — and Thales makes yes and no sum to 1.

The flat diameter is the 0° sheet, the coin flip. Turned 90° it is the 45° sheet. Every angle on the dial is a diameter too.

SCENE 13 · 2 OF 2 · WHY THE SIGN HIDES

The 0° sheet is blind by symmetry — the 45° sheet is not

The 0° sheet’s two ends sit on the mirror line. A beam and its twin keep the same distance to both — the same fraction, the same bandwidth size, round after round.

Tilt the diameter and the twins split: the 45° sheet of scene 11, as geometry. The flip is not weak — it is symmetric under exactly the reflection the sign encodes.

A coin owns one question and lives on the half-disk. A sheet on a dial owns them all and lives on the circle.

(ii) Half of what? Of a circle: every beam has a mirror twin.

ACT III · SCENES 14–16

A block of glass — the disk becomes a ball

no stripes · nothing lost · a third question · Poincaré’s sphere

the same beams — one more question to ask them

SCENE 14 · 1 OF 5 · THE BENCH WE KNOW

Striped light: sheet 2 finds the direction

The sunglasses bench. Sheet 1 diagonal, sheet 2 diagonal: 100%.

Turn sheet 2 horizontal: 50%. Crossed: 0%. Somewhere, the light is all through; somewhere, none.

Nothing new. Remember the lamp alone: 50% at every angle, no direction to find. Two signatures — hold both.

SCENE 14 · 2 OF 5 · A GLASS THAT TURNS

Turning the light without asking it anything

Sheet 1 diagonal. Between the sheets a block of glass, no stripes. Turn sheet 2: the whole pattern of scene 7 — turned by 90°.

And the beam is as bright behind the glass as before it. The third sheet turned the direction by asking, and kept a quarter; the glass turns it by transforming.

A second glass turns it back, as if nothing stood there. A turn can be undone; a sheet’s loss cannot.

SCENE 14 · 3 OF 5 · A BLOCK OF GLASS · FRESNEL 1817

The glass: a direction that reads like lamplight

Same bench, sheet 1 diagonal. The same glass, now a quarter of the turn: still no stripes, nothing absorbed, the beam as bright as before.

Turn sheet 2: 50%, 50%, 50%. Sheet 2 can no longer find the direction sheet 1 gave — though nothing was lost.

The surprise. Lamplight’s answer from a beam that had a direction — and the glass asked nothing, took nothing.

SCENE 14 · 4 OF 5 · THE TEST · FRESNEL 1823✳ AHA MOMENT

A second glass brings the direction back

“The glass scrambled it — made it lamplight.” Test that: add a second glass, built the other way round.

Turn sheet 2: 100%, 50%, 0%. The direction is back. Now the same with lamplight: 50% — still 50%.

Scrambling cannot be undone. This could. The light after one glass only looked like lamplight.

SCENE 14 · 5 OF 5 · WHAT THE GLASS SHOWS · STOKES 1852CLAIM № 4 · SIGHTED

The direction did not vanish — it became a timing

Light · wave: an H wiggle and a V wiggle, both still there, full size.

Behind the glass the V wiggle runs a quarter turn late. In step: a line. Out of step: the tip goes round — any sheet keeps half of a circle.

A striped sheet sees how big the two parts are, never when they peak. Two things to know about the light now — the disk has room for one. Stokes (1852) kept a third tally for it.

SCENE 16 · THE BALL · POINCARÉ 1892CLAIM № 3 · REDEEMED✳ AHA MOMENT

Two things on one point — add an axis. Third time: the ball

A coin at ½ twice over gave us the bandwidth axis. An H-beam through the floor unfolded the disk.

Tilt: lamplight stays at the centre — inside. The glass-light rises to the pole — on the surface; its twin to the other pole.

Same 50% from every striped sheet, two different places on the ball. Pure on the surface, mixed inside. Bernoulli, Poincaré, Bloch — one ball.

SCENE 16 · ROUND A ON THE BALL · THE FAIR BEAM

The fair beam: top of the rim, flat on the disk

Three questions per beam now: the 0° sheet, the 45° sheet, and reversed glass + 45° sheet.

The fair beam climbs to the top of the rim, as the fair coin did.

Tilt the disk: it stays on the equator. The third question answers ½ — a linear beam has no third number.

SCENE 16 · ROUND B ON THE BALL · THE MYSTERY BEAM

The mystery beam: corners — the room at the centre

Every seat: H or V, nobody knows which. Each path shoots to a corner; the room pools to the centre.

Tilt the disk: the room does not move. The centre is inside the ball.

A mixture — lamplight’s place. Keep your eye on ‘inside’ for the next two rounds.

SCENE 16 · ROUND C ON THE BALL · THE SECRET BEAM, SEALED

The secret beam, sealed: inside the disk — until you tilt

Every seat: a sealed beam — a secret, and no word on how it was made. The seats land inside the disk, short of the rim. Mixtures, surely.

Tilt the disk: every seat lifts to the surface. Pure from the start — the disk only showed their shadow.

Only the room stays inside: it doesn’t know which seat is which. Seat-pure, room-mixed.

SCENE 16 · ROUND C ON THE BALL · THE CIRCULAR BEAM

The circular beam: the centre too — until you tilt

The fair beam through the glass. 0° sheet: ½. 45° sheet: ½. Every seat at the centre — the mystery room, to a striped sheet.

Tilt the disk: the third question answers 1. The whole room is at the pole, on the surface.

The sealed round’s extreme case: a shadow dead on the mystery room’s point — two lights, two heights.

The secret the coin had no room for: fair to every sheet, yet fully known.

SCENE 16 · THE NEEDLE IN FULL · JONES 1941CLAIM № 2 · NAMED

The needle, in full: the wave behind the ball

Scene 5’s needle, (√p, √(1−p)), runs on a unit circle at angle α; the pointer at 2α. Squaring doubles the angle: sheet 45°, disk 90° — derived.

For light the needle is the wave: a and b are the sizes of the H and V wiggles; the glass adds their delay δ. (a, b·eiδ) is Jones’s vector: δ = 0 the equator, a quarter turn the poles.

Only the relation between the parts is physical: a sign on the disk, a delay on the ball. One lap, (a, b) → (−a, −b): the same beam. Cox’s little quantities: amplitudes.

Each of you is one photon. Press once per round.

Four rounds of φ: the room's own clicks draw the fringe. A mixture source draws nothing.

One click at a time — and still cos²(φ/2). Each photon carries the whole ball.

SCENE 18 · TWO PHOTONS · 1 OF 2✳ BEYOND THE LADDER · OPTIONAL

Which two of you are entangled?

Two seats in this room left one source together. Neither knows it. Alone, each is a fair coin at every angle.

The only detector is a comparison. Each round, everyone gets the same sheet; every pair that disagrees is struck.

276 pairs, halved each round — until two remain. Two?

SCENE 18 · TWO PHOTONS · 2 OF 2✳ BEYOND THE LADDER · OPTIONAL

Correlated — or entangled?

Two pairs survived. One is entangled. The other shares a card, written before any question was asked.

Now ask them different angles — 0°, 60°, 120°. A card with three answers always has two alike: the same answer at least ⅓ of the time.

The photons: cos²(60°) = ¼. No card can be written for that — and no single seat ever showed it.

SCENE 19 · ENCORE

The room decoheres

One pure beam. Each participant bumps their delay by a random amount.

Every copy is still pure; the room's average is not.

That is what a noisy room does to a qubit. Thank you for being the noise.

WORKING NOTES · NOT FOR THE ROOM✳ ITERATE HERE

Towards gates: what the deck already has, what it still needs

HAVEGlass = one-qubit gate. Half-wave at 45°: X. Half-wave at 22.5°: Hadamard. Quarter-wave: S (equator → pole). Any one-qubit unitary = QWP·HWP·QWP — Euler angles on the ball.
HAVESheet = measurement. Asking throws away and cannot be undone (scene 14c); glass transforms and can (scene 14d). The two primitives of the circuit model, already contrasted.
HAVEEntanglement exists (scene 18): the two-qubit resource, shown by its statistics only — not yet by an operation.
NEEDThe two-qubit gate. Glass acts on each photon alone; a CNOT needs one photon’s state to turn the other’s. Photons do not do this by themselves (KLM 2001: measurement + extra photons).
IDEASTwo sheets on one dial (a “controlled sheet”)? · Scene 18 run backwards: build the pair instead of finding it · a two-coin ledger kept honestly, as scene 5 did for one · the “which end?” question asked of a pair.
ALSOGate dictionary slide after “the needle in full”, on the ball figure (delay dial already there). One line on the receipt: every single-qubit gate is, geometrically, a wave plate.
